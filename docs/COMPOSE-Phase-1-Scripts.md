# **COMPOSE: Phase 1 Auditory Anchor Scripts (Days 1–25)**

**Phase 1 Focus:** The Autonomic Reset

**Duration:** 25 Days

**Target Audio Length:** 2–3 Minutes per track

## **Voiceover Delivery Instructions**

* **Tone:** Deliver the audio with a deep, calm, slow, and unhurried masculine voice.  
* **Resonance:** Settle your pitch into your chest.  
* **Pacing:** Pause for a full 2 seconds wherever an ellipsis (...) appears. Do not rush.

### **Day 1: The Sovereign Presence**

**Core Focus:** Overcoming "Spectatoring" and performance evaluation.

"Welcome to Day 1 of your protocol. Settle into your body... take a slow, deep breath in through your nose... and release it completely. Right now, your brain treats intimacy like an exam you have to pass. When a sexual encounter begins, you mentally step out of your own skin. You float up into your head, watching yourself perform. You ask, 'Is this working? Am I hard enough? Am I going to finish too fast?'

In clinical science, this is called spectatoring. It is not a physical failure. It is an evolutionary safety mechanism. Your brain senses performance pressure, mistakes it for danger, and floods your system with adrenaline. Adrenaline constricts blood vessels and accelerates your reflexes.

The spectator is what causes the exact failure you are trying to avoid. Today, your only objective is to step off the stage. There is no audience. There is no grade. When you catch yourself evaluating your body today, simply notice the thought, let it float by like static, and bring your focus entirely to the physical sensation of your feet on the floor. You are present. You are grounded. You are safe."

### **Day 2: Neutralizing the Perfect Metric**

**Core Focus:** Dismantling "All-or-Nothing" thinking patterns regarding erection quality.

"Welcome to Day 2\. Settle in, drop your shoulders, and let your breathing drop into your stomach. There is a powerful cultural myth that a healthy male body operates like a light switch—that once you are physically aroused, you must remain at one hundred percent rigidity until the moment of release. This is biologically false.

Intimacy has natural waves. Blood flow ebbs, flows, softens, and firms based on changes in touch, position, and focus. But right now, if your body softens for even five seconds, your brain catastrophizes. It panics. That panic floods your system with vasoconstricting hormones, turning a natural biological fluctuation into a permanent adrenaline lock.

Today, we neutralize the metric. If your body softens, it is not a catastrophe, and it does not mean you are broken. It means your body is breathing. Allow the wave. Do not fight it. When you remove the panic of losing an erection, you remove the very anxiety that causes you to lose it."

### **Day 3: The Pelvic Drop**

**Core Focus:** Introducing Pelvic Floor Muscle down-training and Reverse Kegels.

"Welcome to Day 3\. Today, we connect the mind to your pelvic muscles. When your nervous system is anxious, your body unconsciously clenches. You might feel it in your jaw, your neck, or your shoulders. But most crucially, you clench your pelvic floor—the sling of muscles between your pubic bone and tailbone. This chronic tightness, or hypertonicity, acts as a physical hair-trigger for your ejaculatory reflex.

Today, we practice the Pelvic Drop. Take a deep, slow inhale down into your stomach, letting your abdomen expand. As you inhale, imagine gently sending that air all the way down into your pelvis, softening and dropping the muscles between your legs. It should feel like a gentle downward release—widening the space between your sit bones. This drop is the physical brake pedal for your nervous system. Repeat this drop three times today at your desk or in your car. Inhale, expand, and drop."

### **Day 4: The Stopwatch Trap**

**Core Focus:** Dismantling time-driven performance metrics.

"Welcome to Day 4\. Today, we address the clock. Society conditions men to believe that the ultimate measure of their intimate value is the stopwatch—how many minutes they can endure before climax. This turning of the bedroom into a timed performance is what triggers your performance anxiety. Physiological latency is a natural byproduct of a relaxed, parasympathetic state—not a product of mental willpower. When you try to 'force' yourself to last longer, your autonomic system interprets that force as stress, clenches your pelvic muscles, and actually accelerates you toward the finish line.

Your value as a partner is anchored in your presence, your touch, and your connection—not a countdown timer. Today, give yourself permission to drop the clock. Intimacy is a landscape to be felt, not a race to be run."

### **Day 5: The Autonomic Window**

**Core Focus:** Understanding the neurobiology of consecutive intimacy.

"Welcome to Day 5\. Breathe in deep... and let it go. If you struggle with early release, you have likely noticed a frustrating pattern: the first encounter after a multi-day break is almost impossible to control, but if you attempt a second round shortly after, or try again the next day, your control is vastly superior.

This is not a fluke. It is the Autonomic Window. Following ejaculation, your brain undergoes a neurochemical shift, releasing prolactin which naturally dampens the physical sensitivity of your genital nerves. Furthermore, the initial exposure has successfully burned off your baseline performance adrenaline.

Do not view an early release as a failure. View it as clearing the adrenaline. The consecutive encounter is your somatic training ground. It is where your nervous system is calm enough to learn what true control actually feels like."

### **Day 6: Sensation Over Evaluation**

**Core Focus:** Shifting attention from cognitive worry to tactile feedback.

"Welcome to Day 6\. Your performance anxiety cannot survive in the immediate present moment. It requires the future to exist. It relies on 'what ifs.' What if I fail? What if my partner is disappointed? Today's clinical tool is sensory displacement. You cannot analyze your erection and feel tactile pleasure at the exact same time. When you are close to your partner, or practicing solo today, the moment your brain boots up its evaluation software, I want you to instantly shift 100% of your attention to physical touch.

Feel the exact temperature of her skin. Notice the texture of her hair. Focus on the warmth of your hands. Sensation over evaluation. You starve the anxiety loop by redirecting your attention."

### **Day 7: The Zero-Goal Principle**

**Core Focus:** Introducing Sensate Focus Stage 1\.

"Welcome to Day 7\. One week of systematic rewriting. The fastest way to disarm bedroom performance anxiety is to remove the performance entirely. This is the clinical foundation of Sensate Focus. It means engaging in physical touch with an absolute, pre-agreed rule: there is no goal. Penetration, genital release, and the expectation of erections are completely off the table.

When the threat of failure is removed, your sympathetic fight-or-flight response disarms. And when the pressure to perform disappears, your body's natural erectile and pacing mechanisms are allowed to function.

Today, consider suggesting this to your partner. Ask for a session of pure, non-demand touch. Reclaim the pleasure of touch without the weight of a goal."

### **Day 8: Dismantling the Shame Loop**

**Core Focus:** Breaking the post-falter silence with pre-planned scripts.

"Welcome to Day 8\. Let's address the silence. When an intimate encounter doesn't go as planned, most men react by pulling away. We go quiet. We stare at the ceiling. We let our inner critic tell us we are broken and alone. But most men experience these exact fluctuations. The silence is what gives the shame its power. Having a simple, pre-planned script ready—something like: 'My body is feeling highly excited tonight. Let's slow down and focus on touch while my system resets' —completely shatters the shame loop. Own the moment, drop your pelvic floor, and stay in the room."

### **Day 9: Riding the Adrenaline Wash**

**Core Focus:** Autonomic de-sensitization to adrenaline spikes.

"Welcome to Day 9\. Take a slow, diaphragmatic breath... expand your abdomen... and let it fall. You cannot stop the initial flash of performance anxiety. When intimacy approaches, your brain will likely trigger a wave of adrenaline. Your job is not to prevent the wave. Your job is to stop bracing against it.

When you feel that familiar rush of nervous energy in your chest, do not panic about the panic. Recognize it simply as a misplaced autonomic alarm system. Say to yourself, 'There is the adrenaline.' Then, consciously execute a Pelvic Drop and breathe slow. Let the adrenaline wash pass through you. It only stays locked in your body if you try to fight it."

### **Day 10: The Arousal Plateau**

**Core Focus:** Using the 1-to-10 PONR scale to map internal pacing.

"Welcome to Day 10\. Today we map your somatic pacing. We do not use stopwatches; we use the Arousal Scale. Imagine your physical excitement on a scale from 1, which is completely neutral, to 10, which is the Point of No Return. Anxious men often jump from a level 3 directly to a 9 in seconds because their pelvic floor is clenched tight. Your objective in the coming weeks is to learn the landscape of the 6 and the 7\. During solo practice or partner touch, identify your exact physical markers at a level 7\. When you hit an 8, use your breath and a deep pelvic drop to bring it back to a 6\. Mastery is learning to ride the plateau."

### **Day 11: The Mind-Reading Trap**

**Core Focus:** Eliminating cognitive projections of partner disappointment.

"Welcome to Day 11\. One of the most destructive cognitive errors we make in our relationships is Mind Reading. It is the absolute certainty that you know exactly what your partner is thinking—and that she is judging you, disappointed in you, or tensing with frustration. You cannot read minds. You are projecting your own internal performance anxiety onto your partner. Most partners are far more attuned to your emotional presence, your warmth, and your desire for them than the stopwatch metrics of your erection. When you assume she is judging you, you pull away, creating the very distance you fear. Stay in your body, and trust the connection."

### **Day 12: Somatic Capillary Flow**

**Core Focus:** Introducing low-excitation vascular retraining.

"Welcome to Day 12\. Let's look at how we train vascular flow. Traditional high-speed, high-friction solo habits train the nervous system to associate physical touch with an immediate sprint to ejaculation. To reverse this conditioning, we use low-excitation Capillary Flow training. This means engaging in slow, gentle manual touch while keeping your arousal strictly at a level 4 or 5\. Do not aim for a full erection. Do not aim for climax. The goal is simply to allow warm, steady blood flow to expand your pelvic tissues while keeping your nervous system completely calm. You are teaching your body that touch does not equal urgency."

### **Day 13: Anchoring the Breath**

**Core Focus:** Breathing as the ultimate autonomic master switch.

"Welcome to Day 13\. When your mind races, your breath shortens. It moves up into your chest, becoming shallow and rapid. This chest-breathing signals to your brain that you are under threat, triggering adrenaline and pelvic floor clenching. Your breath is your anchor. It is the only autonomic function in your body that you can consciously control. By forcing your breath down into your diaphragm, expanding your stomach, and slowing your exhale, you physically force your heart rate to drop. You cannot have a panic attack while taking slow, deep diaphragmatic breaths. Let the breath be your master switch."

### **Day 15: The Myth of Spontaneity**

**Core Focus:** Dismantling the Hollywood ideal that intimacy must be entirely spontaneous.

"Welcome to Day 15\. Settle into your breath... inhale deeply... and release. ### **Day 16: Sensory Grounding**

**Core Focus:** An emergency brake for acute spectatoring using the 5-4-3-2-1 technique.

"Welcome to Day 16\. Today, we install an emergency brake. Despite your best efforts, there will be moments when spectatoring takes over. You will suddenly find yourself floating above the bed, evaluating your body, and feeling the adrenaline spike.

When this happens, do not fight the thoughts. Instead, immediately override your brain's processing center with sensory data. This is clinical grounding. Find three things you can physically see in the room—the shadow on the wall, the texture of the blanket. Find two things you can physically feel—the weight of your partner, the temperature of the air. Find one thing you can hear—her breathing, or the hum of the air conditioner.

Anxiety requires you to be in the future. Sensory data forces your brain back into the present second. See it. Feel it. Hear it. Ground yourself."

### **Day 17: Normalizing the Soft Reset**

**Core Focus:** Redefining the temporary loss of an erection as a biological reset, not a failure.

"Welcome to Day 17\. Drop your shoulders, and breathe into your stomach. Let's talk about the moment things soften. For an anxious man, losing rigidity mid-encounter feels like falling off a cliff. Panic sets in, the jaw clenches, and the mental script screams, 'It’s happening again.'

The only thing keeping it away is your fear."

**Core Focus:** Shifting from cognitive/visual fantasy to physical presence.

COMPOSE does not teach edging. We teach plateauing. Your goal is not to flirt with the edge of the cliff. Your goal is to comfortably set up camp at a level 6 or 7\. Smooth, controlled, and relaxed. Stop sprinting to the edge. Learn to master the middle."

### **Day 22: Communicating the Pause**

"Welcome to Day 23\. Let's look at how you define victory. Right now, your brain likely categorizes intimacy in black and white: penetration leading to climax is a success, and anything else is a failure.
