Right now, your brain treats intimacy like an exam you have to pass. When a sexual encounter begins, you mentally step out of your own skin. You float up into your head, watching yourself perform. You ask, 'Is this working? Am I hard enough? Am I going to finish too fast?'

In clinical science, this is called spectatoring. It is not a physical failure. It is an evolutionary safety mechanism. Your brain senses performance pressure, mistakes it for danger, and floods your system with adrenaline. Adrenaline constricts blood vessels and accelerates your reflexes.

The spectator is what causes the exact failure you are trying to avoid. Today, your only objective is to step off the stage. There is no audience. There is no grade. When you catch yourself evaluating your body today, simply notice the thought, let it float by like static, and bring your focus entirely to the physical sensation of your feet on the floor. You are present. You are grounded. You are safe.
