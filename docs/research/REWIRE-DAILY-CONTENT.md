## 1. Design decisions and the psychology behind them

**Why one quote per day, fixed to the protocol day (not random).** Determinism is already the app's law (§7), but it also does psychological work: a fixed Day-40 quote means every user hits "…their greatest success came just one step beyond the point at which defeat had overtaken them" at the exact center of the motivation trough, where churn risk peaks. Randomization would spend the best material on days that don't need it. The quote is the day's *external voice* — borrowed authority from a 1937 text with zero clinical shame-load — and the sequence runs the same arc as the protocol itself: Phase 1 quotes all say "this state is controllable" (agency without demand), Phase 2 quotes introduce decision/persistence/reframed-defeat (manageable challenge as exposure stimulus), Phase 3 quotes go first-person and identity-level. The final week deliberately repeats the master-of-fate motif three times (Days 69, 72, 75) as a crescendo, ending on the one line worth memorizing for life.

**Why exactly 3 statements, in fixed triads.** Three is the ceiling before an affirmation block becomes a list the user skims instead of a ritual he performs (working-memory chunking; also Hick's-Law-adjacent — no scanning, no choosing). The 75 statements are grouped into 25 fixed *triads* rather than drawn independently, so each trio becomes a small liturgy — the statements inside a triad reinforce one theme and are always encountered together, which builds compound associations ("I am disciplined / I am consistent / I feel the power and clarity…" lands as one belief, not three).

**Why each triad appears exactly once per phase.** 75 days × 3 slots = 225 slots = each of the 75 statements exactly 3 times, once per phase. A ~25-day revisit interval is spaced repetition at the range where re-encounter feels like *recognition* ("I've said this before — and it's truer now") rather than repetition fatigue. Same statement, new phase, more accumulated behavioral evidence behind it — that evidence gap between readings is what converts a sentence into a self-belief (self-perception: he's watched himself act like the statement for 25 days since he last read it).

**Why the statements are behaviorally anchored.** Most of the pool (Andrew's originals set the pattern) asserts *behavior and stance* — "I do hard things," "I keep the promises I make to myself" — not raw outcomes ("I am confident"). Outcome affirmations can backfire in low-self-worth states (they generate counter-argument); behavior-anchored ones can't be argued with once the daily loop supplies the evidence. The check-in placement is the trick: he reads "I show up on the days I don't feel like it" *in the act of showing up*. The negative-framed ones ("I do not rush anxiously…") are kept deliberately — "I don't" phrasing is empirically stronger refusal language than "I can't" because it's an identity claim, not a rule.

**Ritual mechanics recommendation (UI).** Statements reveal one at a time, advance on tap — the micro-interaction is a commitment device and forces one reading per statement (generation effect beats passive display). Quote sits above in Newsreader; statements in Newsreader italic (the oath/mirror-sentence register per §6). Attribution line in micro-type: "Napoleon Hill, Think and Grow Rich (1937)" — public-domain provenance is a trust asset. No streaks, no urgency copy anywhere on this surface.

**Legal:** all 75 quotes verbatim from the public-domain 1937 edition (see `docs/research/HILL-QUOTE-LIBRARY.md` for provenance and the excluded-material list). No money/riches-vocabulary quotes are used (machine-checked). Day 68 is deliberately a partial quote with trailing ellipsis. Before launch, spot-check the 75 against a 1937 facsimile and log page numbers here.
